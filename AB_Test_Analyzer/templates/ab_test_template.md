# A/B Test Template

## Problem
Create a detailed representation of the problem that supports the rationale for running an experiment. Align the problem with your business KPIs using quantitative and qualitative data from multiple sources (charts, acceptance testing, customer feedback, heat mapping, and other analytic sources).

## Create a Hypothesis
A good hypothesis is solution-oriented and contains cause, effect, and rationale:
1. **Cause:** "By adding a new "Invite Friends" CTA on the homepage
2. **Effect:** "we will observe an increase in social engagement, and therefore an increase in class bookings"
3. **Rationale:** "because more users will be more engaged with our product".

## Predict a Result:
Identify and quantify the change to expect to see in your metrics as a result of the experiment:
1. **Primary Metric**: What is the most important metric you want to impact? Should it increase or decrease?
2. **Specify MDE (Minimal Detectable Effect)**: What percent change (MDE) do you expect?
3. **Secondary Metrics**: What secondary metric(s) do you need to move or monitor?
4. **Guardrails**: What metrics should be monitored to avoid things to break up?

## Determine the scope of the Exposure
Define a target strategy that includes (at minimum):
1. **Variants:** The option being tested on your audience. The change you are introducing to the current (control) experience.
2. **Targeting and Allocation:** The percent or number of targeted users you want to be exposed to a particular variant (it could be a region and how you want to split them).
3. **Timelines:** How much time the test should last?

## Analysis: 
List out specific questions you hope to answer with your analysis.

## Future Work 
Decide what to do once the experiment is over, based on the potential analysis and outcomes of your experiment:
1. **Significant results (variant success):** Determine how you will roll out this experience. Will you do a gradual rollout? What groups of users will be a part of the rollout? 
2. **Significant results (variant failure):** Dig into your results and find out what went well, what was learned, and what could have been done differently. Decide if you should make adjustments and run another test or not.
3. **Non-significant results:** If you get non-significant results, will you re-test? Do you have the time and resources to do so? What adjustments are you willing to make? What adjustments are you unwilling to make? If you are not going to re-test, how will you use your learnings from this experiment?

## What Do We Expect to Learn
Add in the things we will learn after the experiment is finished.
